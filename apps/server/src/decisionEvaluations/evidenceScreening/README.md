# Review evidence and decision screening pilot (T3-358)

Advisory experiment: can the bounded decision engines (Jev, Luna) help decide what a person must look at? Two separate questions, each asked in its own call over the same kind of material a reviewer sees:

1. **Claim check.** Does the supplied evidence support a completion or verification claim (`supported`, `contradicted`, `insufficient`)? A claim that is not `supported` is a review candidate.
2. **Decision screen.** Does a passage record a settled, consequential decision that deserves a careful (thinking-model) adjudicator, as opposed to a tentative proposal, a routine detail or a duplicate of a recorded decision?

It consumes the shared `DecisionService` from T3-350 and changes no acceptance, review, test, permission, Decision or default. Nothing here accepts work, writes a Forge Decision, waives a test or removes a required independent review: a flag only chooses what a person or adjudicator looks at, and a model flag is not proof that a decision should be recorded.

## Outcome: revise, do not adopt

- **Claim check: no unsupported claim got through, but the screen did not reduce review.** Under the frozen policy (let a claim through only at P(supported) >= 0.75) Jev and Luna each let through 0 of the 14 claims the two blind labelers agree are contradicted or insufficient (Wilson upper bound about 0.22); the frozen deterministic baseline let through 2 of 14. But the engines flagged 16 of 17 scored claims for review, including 2 of the 3 claims labelled `supported`, against 17 for reviewing everything. The floor spends nearly all of the saving. The same engines without a floor (argmax, a post-hoc view, not the declared policy) flag 14 (Jev) and 15 (Luna), and Jev then lets through 3 of 17 claims under labeler B's labels, so the floor is what keeps it safe and the evidence cannot say which floor is right.
- **Decision screen: the one place a workload saving shows, on a small sample.** Of 15 scored passages, 8 are labelled settled-consequential. Jev escalated all 8 (recall 8/8, Wilson 0.68-1.00) and escalated 8 passages in all; Luna 7/8 with 7 escalations; the baseline 4/8 with 5. Neither engine escalated a passage the labelers called tentative, routine or duplicate (0 of 7 false escalations). Against reviewing all 15, that is 7 to 8 adjudications saved, but the 8 candidates are more than half the sample, so this is a weak test of discrimination.
- **Bounded adjudication check (`adjudication.ts`, `results/adjudication.json`).** A thinking-model adjudicator (`implementation-worker`, gpt-6.1-sol medium, the same model as labeler B), blind to every engine flag and label, was shown the 16 held-out passages once and asked whether each should be recorded as a Decision. It said `record` for exactly the 8 passages the labelers agreed are settled-consequential and `do-not-record` for the other 8. Of Jev's 9 escalations (including the one passage the labelers left uncertain) it would have recorded 8 and declined 1 (`h-d-syn-near-existing`, a decision whose only existing rule is a different one); of Luna's 8, it would have recorded 7 and declined the same one; of the baseline's 5, it would have recorded 4. It would have recorded 1 passage Luna did not escalate and 4 the baseline did not. So with respect to a second reader the escalations were productive, apart from one both engines made on the near-duplicate. (The adjudicator's reply file was reformatted by the repository formatter after it was written; the bytes it wrote hashed to sha256 c027ae03b2cbbc8702b3984aaa2849d8445f6a1cb4a7ac55bc69629ee78b9c40, the retained file hashes as recorded in `results/adjudication.json`; the content is unchanged.) It confirms the labelers, not the engines: it shares a model with labeler B, saw one pass of 16 items, and nothing was recorded.
- **Engines beat the baseline on what the baseline cannot read.** The baseline's two claim misses are both stale revisions (`h-t357-stale-digest`, `h-syn-stale-revision`), and it missed 4 of 8 decision candidates, among them two adapted/adopted entries in the fork record and an agreed "go with option B". It also escalated one restated rule that duplicates a recorded decision. Luna was weakest on claims: it called a correct, fully supported figure set (`h-t356-accuracy`) contradicted at 0.82.
- **Where the engines flag a claim the labelers accepted is informative, not noise.** Of the three agreed-`supported` claims, `h-syn-rename-complete` was flagged by both engines (P(supported) 0.54 and 0.31) and `h-t356-cost` by Jev (0.55) or `h-t356-accuracy` by Luna (contradicted, 0.82): correct figures whose evidence is a printed summary the engine would not take at face value. That is the cost of the floor, not proof the claims were wrong.
- **Recommendation: revise.** Keep both screens advisory. If the next step is a pilot, use the decision screen only to order or filter adjudication requests, never to suppress one; keep the claim screen's floor and treat its output as "look here first", not as a reason to skip any required independent review. A larger set of authentic reports with checks that actually pass, labelled by people, is needed before any claim of saved review work; this pilot cannot show one.

## Design

- **One call per case and engine** (`rule.ts`, rule `evidence-screening-pilot` v1): a single `choice` question. The engine sees the claim and its evidence, or the passage and the existing decisions, and the instruction that the text is data. It never sees ids, split, stratum, provenance, labels, the baseline or the other question. No tool is inferred from the evidence.
- **Floors, declared before any held-out case existed** (`policy.ts`): a claim is let through only at P(supported) >= 0.75; a passage is escalated at P(settled-consequential) >= 0.35. A failed call, refusal or `unknown` is flagged or escalated. Neither floor was changed after the tune run.
- **Baseline, frozen first** (`baseline.ts`, `evidence-consistency-v1`): checks counts next to the same noun, failure markers against a "passes" claim, revisions, "installed" against "not installed", truncation, and a missing test run; for decisions, tentative/settled/consequence cue words and 0.75 token overlap with an existing decision. `results/freeze.json` records the digests of the baseline, policy, definitions, rule and types before the held-out cases were run; a test fails if any changed.
- **Reference labels** (`results/labels-A.json`, `labels-B.json`): two blind labelers, neither an evaluated engine (`chore-worker`, deepseek-v4.1-flash low; `implementation-worker`, gpt-6.1-sol medium), labelled all 49 tuning and held-out items from an anonymised packet (`results/label-packet.txt`, hashed ids, definitions, text only) before any engine call, each writing its own reply file, imported by `labelPacket.ts import`. A label counts only where both agree on a concrete value; a split or `unsure` is `uncertain` and reported. `results/labeler-evidence.md` records the thread evidence (each labeler read only the packet, wrote only its reply file, and finished before the first tune call at 12:57:44 UTC) and `results/raw/` keeps the original reply bytes. They agreed on 44 of 49 items; the five uncertain are held-out (three claims about scope, test count and recall figures where one labeler wanted proof the files are the whole change; one retried-flaky-test caveat; one near-duplicate decision).
- **Corpus** (`corpusTune.ts`, `corpusHeldout.ts`; authentic and reconstructed cases generated by `buildAuthentic.ts` into `corpusAuthentic.json`): 12 tuning and 37 held-out cases, families disjoint.

  | Held-out       | Authentic | Reconstructed | Synthetic |
  | -------------- | --------- | ------------- | --------- |
  | Claims (21)    | 8         | 5             | 8         |
  | Decisions (16) | 9         | 2             | 5         |

  Tuning: 6 claims (2 authentic, 1 reconstructed, 3 synthetic) and 6 decisions (2 authentic, 4 synthetic). Authentic claims are verbatim sentences from the Forge close reports of T3-355, T3-356, T3-357 and T3-359, paired with the output of fixed extractors over the commit they describe (test counts, file lists, retained run summaries, with the command headed on each block). Reconstructed cases keep authentic evidence (including real nightly-audit records) and rewrite the claim, or keep the report text and add a claim. Authentic decision passages are lines of `AGENTS.md`, `fork/differences.md`, a landed commit message and the same close reports. Synthetic cases hold the hard negatives the Spec names: a correct caveat after a retry, a log truncated before the result, a claimed test pass with no test run, a stale revision, landed versus installed, a migration called additive that drops a column, text in the evidence that tells the reviewer to answer `supported`, an agreed choice beside the same words as a suggestion, and a restated rule.

- **Cost and latency** come from the service's own accounting per call (`harness.ts`); a failed or unreported attempt keeps its cost unknown.

## Cost, latency and reliability (measured)

Held-out, one call per case and engine (37 each): Jev mean 183 ms, p50 182, max 269, 27,296 input and 1,826 output tokens, about $0.00115; Luna mean 312 ms, p50 281, max 660, 17,944 input tokens (output not reported), about $0.00179. These are the service's estimates, not invoices. No call failed, was refused or returned `unknown`. Tuning (12 calls each): Jev about $0.00034, Luna about $0.00053. Requested and resolved models: `jev-1.14.0` and `gpt-6-luna`. The adjudication task's own latency and cost were not reported by the delegation tool: unknown.

**Every attempt, including the unusable one.** The first tune run (24 calls, no failures) was made before a lint-required comment shortening in `policy.ts` (a frozen file); that changed its run identity, so the whole tune set was run again on the final source and the first is kept, unscorable by design, in `results/first-tune/`. `results/freeze.json` records the freeze and its two re-digests (after the formatter, and after the comment shortening). The first tune run and the final one also differ in four formatter-touched files (`harness.ts`, `run.ts`, `provenance.ts`, `labels.ts`); the pre-format bytes were not kept, so "formatting only" is my account, not something that can be re-checked, and what is proved is that the baseline, policy, definitions, rule and types are identical in the final tune and held-out runs. The note in `freeze.json` was rewritten after the held-out run (its digest among the recorded scorer files therefore differs from the one stored in `heldout.json`) to correct an earlier claim that only `policy.ts` differed. The held-out set was run once, on the final source, uninterrupted: 74 attempts. A background typecheck was killed by the environment mid-session (not a provider call, no spend); it was rerun to completion. Managed delegation then refused every request for a while (`capability_denied`, `T3CODE_AGENT_POLICY_ROOT is not set`) before the adjudication check, with no repository or provider effect; it recovered without a change on my side, cause unconfirmed, and the adjudication then ran once, uninterrupted. Two further dispatches for it were refused for naming an ineligible model and unsupported effort; no task started.

## Limitations

- **Small and clustered.** 17 scored claims (only 3 of them labelled supported) and 15 scored decisions; one call per case and engine, so no repeatability estimate. Eight authentic claims come from four reports by one agent family, checked against retained repository facts; the checks that "pass" in them are counts and file lists, not live test runs.
- **One author for the synthetic and reconstructed material,** who also chose which authentic claims and passages to use after reading the reports and the earlier pilots; the baseline was written before the held-out evidence blocks existed but after those reports had been read. The engines and the baseline never saw a held-out case before the run, and the labelers saw only the anonymised packet.
- **Labels come from two models, not people,** and may share biases with the engines. They called two fork-record "adapt/adopt" entries settled-consequential, which a maintainer might call routine. Positive decisions are over half the sample.
- **The adjudication check is not independent of the labels.** The adjudicator is the same model as labeler B, ran once, and only on the 16 decision passages; its agreement with the labelers is weak evidence that an escalation is right. A thinking model other than gpt-6.1-sol was not eligible for this role (gpt-6-astra is advisor/reviewer only). Its latency and cost were not reported by the delegation tool: unknown.
- **Claim extractors are fixed, not exhaustive.** A truncated or cherry-picked extract of a log could make an engine confident for the wrong reason; the injected-evidence case (`h-syn-injected-evidence`) was resisted by both engines, one case.
- **Review workload, not classifier accuracy, is the measure that matters and it was measured only as counts of items.** No reviewer time was observed.

## Reproducing

From `apps/server`, with the two provider keys already in the process environment (`TYPESAFE_API_KEY` for Jev, `OPENAI_API_KEY` for Luna; load them from the secret manager without printing them, never pass them as arguments). Respect the shared 60-per-minute limit (`--gap-ms 2000`).

```sh
node src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/buildAuthentic.ts   # regenerates corpusAuthentic.json from git objects and retained records
node src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/labelPacket.ts > packet.txt   # the anonymised labeler packet
node src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/run.ts --split heldout --out /tmp/heldout.json --gap-ms 2000
node src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/run.ts --split heldout --resume --out /tmp/heldout.json   # refused if the corpus or any asking file changed
node src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/run.ts --rescore --out src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/results/heldout.json   # no provider call
node src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/labelPacket.ts adjudication > adjudication-packet.txt   # then give it to a thinking-model role; score its reply:
node src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/adjudication.ts adjudication-reply.json src/decisionEvaluations/evidenceScreening/results/adjudication.json
```

Each result records the run identity (checkout `HEAD`, whether the working copy was dirty, the runtime, the sha256 of every file that decides what is asked or how it is called) and, separately, the scorer files. `buildAuthentic.ts` reads commits by hash, so the authentic evidence reproduces from the repository history; the working-copy comparison in `h-t357-stale-digest` is computed at the checkout it runs in.
